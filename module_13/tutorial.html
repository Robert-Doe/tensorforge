Module 13 — k-Means Clustering

Finding case patterns without being told what to look for

01 · The Big Analogy

Detective Ada has 120 case files stacked on her desk. No one has labelled them "solved" or "unsolved" — she just has the raw evidence scores, suspect ages, and witness counts.

She notices some files feel similar to each other — similar crime severity, similar days open, similar evidence. She starts grouping them instinctively into piles. Once a pile has a few files, she puts a sticky note in the middle (the centroid). Then she re-sorts every file to whichever sticky note it's closest to. The sticky notes move. She re-sorts again. Repeat until nothing moves.

That's k-Means.

1
Place k random sticky notes (centroids)
2
Assign each file to nearest centroid
3
Move centroid to mean of its pile
4
Repeat until centroids stop moving

02 · The Algorithm, Step by Step

# Step 1: random initialisation
init_idx   = rng.choice(len(X), size=k, replace=False)
centroids  = X[init_idx].copy()   # k random rows from data

for iteration in range(MAX_ITERATIONS):
    # Step 2: assign — which centroid is each point closest to?
    diff      = X[:, np.newaxis, :] - centroids[np.newaxis, :, :]
    distances = np.sqrt((diff ** 2).sum(axis=2))   # (n_samples, k)
    labels    = np.argmin(distances, axis=1)        # (n_samples,)

    # Step 3: update — move centroids to the mean of their members
    for c in range(k):
        centroids[c] = X[labels == c].mean(axis=0)

    # Step 4: stop if centroids barely moved
    if centroid_shift < TOLERANCE:
        break

03 · Inertia and the Elbow Method

Inertia = sum of squared distances from each point to its assigned centroid. Lower = tighter clusters.

Problem: inertia always decreases as k increases. At k=n (one cluster per point), inertia=0. So you can't just minimise inertia to pick k.

Solution: plot inertia vs k and find the elbow — the point where adding one more cluster gives sharply diminishing returns.

for k in range(2, 11):
    km = KMeans(k=k)
    km.fit(X)
    print(f"k={k}  inertia={km.inertia_:.2f}")
The elbow is not always obvious. On real-world data it often looks like a gentle curve with no sharp bend. In those cases, domain knowledge (or other metrics like silhouette score) should guide your choice of k.

06 · Brain Exercise

Cluster purity is 0.683, but our best supervised model (Gradient Boosting) hit ~0.775 accuracy. Why is the unsupervised result lower?

Does that mean k-Means is worse? Or are they solving different problems?

Answer: They're solving different problems. Gradient Boosting was explicitly trained to predict the "solved" label. k-Means has never seen the labels — it's grouping cases by overall feature similarity, not by solved/unsolved. The fact that it achieves 68% purity without any labels is actually impressive — it means the feature patterns genuinely differ between solved and unsolved cases. Unsupervised methods are most valuable when labels don't exist or are expensive to collect.

07 · Q&A

Q: Why does k-Means require feature scaling?
k-Means uses Euclidean distance. A feature with values 0-365 (days_open) will dominate the distance calculation over a feature with values 0-10 (evidence_score). Scale all features to the same range (e.g., with StandardScaler) so each feature gets an equal vote in the distance.
Q: What is k-means++ and why didn't we use it?
k-means++ initialises centroids by choosing each subsequent centroid with probability proportional to its distance from the nearest already-chosen centroid. This spreads centroids out, dramatically reducing the chance of poor local minima. sklearn's KMeans uses k-means++ by default (init='k-means++'). We used random init here to see the base algorithm clearly.
Q: Can k-Means handle non-spherical clusters?
No. k-Means assumes clusters are roughly spherical and equally sized. Clusters that are elongated, crescent-shaped, or very different in density will confuse it. For those shapes, use DBSCAN (density-based) or Gaussian Mixture Models.
Q: What's the silhouette score?
A per-sample measure: how similar a point is to its own cluster vs. the nearest other cluster. Ranges from -1 (wrong cluster) to +1 (perfect fit). The average across all samples gives a single quality metric. sklearn: from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score.
